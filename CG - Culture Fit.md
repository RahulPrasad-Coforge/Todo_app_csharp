# Article Summaries

**1. Building Swiftee, the AI Learning Companion for Every Learner**

The article describes how ConveGenius is building Swiftee, an AI-powered voice learning companion for children. The team started from one question: how can learning be made easy for a child? ConveGenius calls Swiftee "heartware", meaning it brings to life the devices that parents and schools give children. It works on Android and Windows, across desktops, laptops, tablets and phones. The team spent months on reflection, prototyping and reinvention. Users have enjoyed the warm, expressive Swiftee mascot. The product has 300+ guided activities covering academic and life skills, and this will grow to 800+ in the coming weeks. ConveGenius is now seeking product and program integrations and invites readers to try Swiftee. The article says the future belongs to the AI companion people genuinely love to use, built by listening closely to users on the ground.

**2. From Connectivity to Classrooms: Uttarakhand Launches 50 SwiftSchools**

The article covers Uttarakhand's rollout of the SwiftSchool Model in 50 government schools. An MoU was signed on 25 March 2026 between Samagra Shiksha Uttarakhand and ConveGenius Foundation. The second school launched on 23 May 2026 at GIC Srinagar, Pauri, led by Education Minister Dr Dhan Singh Rawat. Instead of relying on connectivity, the programme uses an offline-first Personalised Adaptive Learning (PAL) solution for rural areas. SwiftSchool combines immersive 3D simulation and interactive content (SwiftClass), a PAL Lab with personalised paths, and a Swift Skills Lab for robotics, 3D printing and design thinking. AWS donated 1,000 laptops through CSR. The state aims to expand to about 2,000 schools, covering roughly 80% of government school students. Programme data will flow into the state's Vidya Samiksha Kendra.

**3. Mission Buniyaad: How Personalised Learning Is Helping Girls Stay in School in Rajasthan**

Mission Buniyaad is a large government-led PAL programme run by the Government of Rajasthan. CIFF funds it, and ConveGenius is the technical partner using SwiftPAL. It has reached nearly 5 lakh girls in about 3,700 government secondary schools. Learning outcomes improved by about 20%, and board exam failure rates fell by 4–6 percentage points. The Government of Rajasthan has approved 1,000 AI-enabled PAL Labs. The article explains why girls face high dropout risk in Rajasthan, including early marriage, household duties and safety concerns. The programme revives dormant ICT labs, uses gender-responsive content, embeds PAL in school timetables and government reviews, and uses dashboards to show who needs help. SwiftPAL finds the exact concept gap in a student's learning and fixes only that. The article presents the results as proof that personalised learning inside government systems can work at scale.
